Privacy Policy

Last updated 5 November 2026

This policy covers the mobile games published by Oisif Games, including Hole City. It describes what the games collect, which third-party services receive it, and what you can ask us to do about it.

We do not ask you to create an account: the game creates an anonymous player account for you automatically. We never ask for your real name, email address, phone number or postal address inside a game.

What we keep ourselves

Oisif Games runs its own server at oisif.io, hosted by Hetzner in Nuremberg, Germany. It receives gameplay events, error logs and any feedback you send. Events carry the install ID, a hashed form of your player ID (not the Cloud ID itself) and the device and app information described above. They never carry the advertising identifier. We use this copy to understand how the game is played, to fix what you report, and to tune the game. A backup is kept on equipment we control, and a deletion reaches it within 30 days.

The game sends identified events and error logs to this server only after you accept the consent card described under Advertising and your choices. That card is about ads. If you refuse it, these are not sent. One anonymous event is sent whatever you answer: the step reached in your first session, with the install ID and the device and app information, and without the player ID. We are still checking with legal advice what basis covers that event.

When you send feedback we store a one-way hash of your IP address to stop abuse, not the address itself. That applies to feedback only. The server also keeps standard web server access logs, which contain the IP address of every request, and we do not currently set a retention period for them that we can state here.

If our server cannot be reached, the game asks francoiscorbel.com, a website run by the developer of Oisif Games, for the current time. That request reaches the site like any web request and so includes your IP address.

We do not sell your personal data for money, and we do not share it with anyone outside the services listed above. Under some US state laws, letting ad partners use identifiers for personalised ads counts as a "sale" or "sharing"; see US residents below.

Crash reports

Crash reports go to Unity through Unity Diagnostics. A report can contain crash and exception logs, telemetry and information about the device and the game version. Unity acts as our processor and identifies the data with its own installation ID, which is not the install ID or Cloud ID described above and which the game does not show you.

Unity keeps this data for 90 days by default. There is no setting in the game to turn crash reporting off, and Unity does not provide a consent option for it. You can write to us to object. Because we cannot look up one player's reports without that Unity ID, we cannot currently delete them on request; they expire on their own after the retention period.

Advertising and your choices

Our games are free and funded by advertising. Ads may be personalised using your device advertising identifier.

  • On iOS, the game asks for permission before any tracking identifier is used. If you decline, or if you have turned off Allow Apps to Request to Track in your device settings, ads are shown without the advertising identifier. The Meta measurement events also stay off.
  • On Android, you can reset or delete your Advertising ID in Settings → Google → Ads.
  • Everywhere, the game shows a consent card about personalised ads after your first run. Personalised ads, the Meta measurement events, and the identified events and error logs we send to our own server run only if you accept. If you refuse, you still get every feature and see non-personalised ads. You can change your answer at any time with the Personalised ads row of the game's settings screen, which carries the label Your privacy choices.

Legal bases (EEA, UK and Switzerland)

  • Consent: personalised advertising, Meta measurement events, showing push notifications, and the identified events and error logs sent to our own server, which the game sends only after you accept the consent card.
  • Performance of a contract: running the game, saving your progress, operating leaderboards and raids, and delivering purchases.
  • Legitimate interests: crash reports to Unity, registering your device with the push service, preventing fraud and abuse, and non-personalised advertising that keeps the game free.

The anonymous first-session event described above is not placed under any of these yet.

How long we keep it

Crash reports at Unity are kept for 90 days by default. Gameplay events and feedback on our own server are kept until you ask us to delete them or until a clean-up removes them; we aim for 24 months, but that clean-up is run by hand and not on a schedule, so some data can stay longer. Saved progress is kept until you delete it, and we do not currently run an automatic deletion of inactive accounts. Our third-party providers apply their own retention periods, described in their policies above.

Where your data goes

Our own server is in Germany. The providers listed above process data on servers in the European Union and the United States. Transfers outside the EEA rely on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses or an adequacy decision, as set out in each provider's policy.

Deleting your data (Hole City and other Oisif Games)

To have your data deleted, email support@oisifgames.com with the subject Data deletion. Quote the install ID and the Cloud ID, which the game shows in Settings (you can copy them from there). The install ID is how we find your gameplay events, so please send both. We delete what we hold within 30 days, including our own copy of your gameplay events and feedback. This also works after you uninstall the game, if you kept the two IDs.

The game has a Delete my data row in Settings. It asks our cloud provider to delete your saved progress, public profile, leaderboard entries, your own raid records and your anonymous player account, and it clears the IDs and local data on your device. It depends on a server step that we have not confirmed is working, so use the email route first. If the game shows Could not delete your data, nothing was deleted. The row also does not reach our own server's events or feedback, and it removes the IDs we ask for in the email from your device, so copy them before you use it. Other players' raid records that name you may keep your player ID.

We cannot currently delete a single player's crash reports held by Unity (see Crash reports); they expire after 90 days.

US residents (California and other states)

Do not sell or share my personal information. To opt out, open the game's Settings and turn off the row Personalised ads, labelled Your privacy choices. When it is off, you see non-personalised ads and we tell Meta to apply Limited Data Use to the events it receives. Turning it off changes nothing else in the game. Apart from that row, the game tells LevelPlay that data is not to be sold for every player, whatever the answer.

You have the right to know what we collect and to get a copy, to delete it, to correct it, to opt out of its sale or sharing, and not to be treated differently for using any of these rights. Use the settings row above, or email support@oisifgames.com with your install ID and Cloud ID. Someone you authorise can make the request for you. We answer within 30 days.

Children

Our games are not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them. If you believe a child has provided us with personal data, write to support@oisifgames.com and we will delete it.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you can ask us to give you a copy of your data, correct it, delete it, restrict or object to how we use it, or withdraw a consent you previously gave. Exercising these rights is free and we answer within 30 days.

Write to support@oisifgames.com. Because player accounts are anonymous, quote the install ID and the Cloud ID shown in the game's settings: that is how we find your record. If we cannot identify you from what you send, we will say so rather than guess.

If you are in the EEA or the UK and you are not satisfied with our response, you can complain to your national data protection authority.

Changes

When this policy changes we update the date at the top of this page. If a change materially affects how we handle your data, we will also say so in the game.
